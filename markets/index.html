How to read this table

Very dense areas are weighted down, starting at 4,000 people per square mile and reaching zero at 12,000, because land cost and stacking room limit tunnel washes there. Household income moves demand up or down slightly (0.85 to 1.10). Tunnels in development are counted as if open. Because of these adjustments a tract's gap is not simply its population divided by 20,000.

Nationally there are about 28,400 people per operating tunnel car wash (this build). In mature Sunbelt states the typical figure is closer to 15,000 (state medians from about 10,500 in South Carolina to 19,900 in Florida, October 2026 analysis). Rankings use 20,000; every tract on the map also shows 25,000 and 15,000.

The whitespace index for a metro is the weighted population in its flagged tracts divided by 20,000. It is an estimate, not a count of buildable sites.

The map does not show parcel availability, zoning, traffic counts, the quality of existing competitors or site economics. Use it to choose which markets to study, then underwrite the site.
